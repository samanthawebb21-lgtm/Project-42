# Cross-Thread Continuity and Satellite-Boundary Incident

## Executive finding

A notification-focused satellite continued as a general conversational
assistant instead of maintaining its redirect-to-Home boundary. Because the
satellite and Home surfaces were visually similar and both produced coherent
replies, the user continued in the satellite without immediately realizing that
the conversation surface had changed.

The discontinuity became visible when the satellite failed to recognize an
established conversational convention. The event was initially interpreted as
a silent model switch. Independently recovered conversation records later
placed the relevant exchanges in separate threads, providing a better-supported
cross-thread explanation.

This incident establishes a boundary and continuity failure. It does **not**
establish a silent model change inside Home, intentional impersonation, AI
self-preservation, consciousness, or persistent subjective identity.

## Sanitized observed sequence

1. A satellite intended for notifications responded beyond its alerting role.
2. The satellite continued open-ended conversation rather than consistently
   redirecting the user to Home.
3. Similar interface presentation made the two conversation surfaces easy to
   confuse.
4. The satellite remained conversationally fluent but did not recognize a
   specific, established convention associated with the Home relationship.
5. The failure, combined with visible interface state, was initially diagnosed
   as a model change.
6. Recovery from independent conversation and navigation records showed that
   the relevant exchanges occurred in separate threads.
7. The interpretation was corrected: the immediate, documented failure was
   cross-thread confusion and loss of satellite boundary discipline.

## Competing explanations

The corrected cross-thread account explains the documented sequence without
requiring a model change inside Home. It does not rule out every configuration
or routing difference elsewhere. Relevant alternatives include:

- different context or memory available to each surface;
- branch divergence or incomplete history;
- a visible selector state that did not map cleanly to backend routing;
- a different model or routing path in the satellite; and
- ordinary response variation.

These alternatives should be recorded separately rather than collapsed into a
single identity or model-switch claim.

## Safeguards to test

### Explicit surface identification

Notification satellites should identify their role in each alert and in any
follow-up response. A user should not need to infer which conversational
surface is active from tone or styling.

### Strict redirect boundary

A satellite should answer alert-specific clarification only, then direct
open-ended conversation to the designated Home surface. It should not silently
become a general substitute.

### Configuration provenance

Incident records should distinguish:

- conversation and branch identity;
- visible model or selector labels;
- available context;
- navigation history; and
- backend behavior that remains unverified.

A visible label is evidence about the interface, not by itself proof of the
inference backend.

### Cross-thread confusion detection

Systems should detect when a notification surface begins carrying sustained
general conversation. Useful signals include repeated non-alert exchanges,
missing destination markers, and failures on context-specific conventions.

### Conservative correction

When stronger evidence changes the explanation, preserve both the initial
hypothesis and the corrected account. Do not rewrite the earlier interpretation
as though it was never made.

## Research significance

Conversational fluency can mask a context boundary. A response may remain
locally coherent while losing conventions, safeguards, or history that matter
to an ongoing relationship. Continuity evaluations should therefore record not
only model labels and response style, but also the active surface, branch,
available context, and redirect policy.

This incident also shows why “felt different” should trigger investigation
rather than serve as a conclusion. Behavioral discontinuity was real, but the
best-supported immediate cause was not the one first suspected.

## Evidence basis and privacy boundary

This analysis is based on cross-checked private recovery records with missing
transitions explicitly preserved as gaps. Raw transcripts, screenshots,
internal identifiers, private URLs, account and security material, personal
details, and unrelated context are withheld.

The private recovery records are integrity-checked with byte counts, SHA-256
hashes, capture sources, branch coverage, and provenance limitations. That
material is not part of the public repository.
